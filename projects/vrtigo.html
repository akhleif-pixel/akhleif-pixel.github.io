Research

VRtigo

Seven bedside eye-movement and balance exams in virtual reality, tested with a stroke patient.

When
Oct 2025 – present
Where
Movement and Plasticity Lab, University of Rochester Medical Center
My role
Undergraduate Research Supervisor, lead developer
  • Unity
  • C#
  • Varjo XR-3
  • Ultraleap
  • Eye tracking

Overview

Dizziness is hard to assess at the bedside: the standard eye-movement and vestibular exams need a trained examiner and specialised goggles. VRtigo puts a battery of seven of those exams into a VR headset with built-in eye tracking, so the same tests can be run and recorded in a consistent way.

It is built in Unity and C# for the Varjo XR-3 headset, with Ultraleap hand tracking. The tests are Test of Nystagmus, Test of Eye, SHIMP, HIMP, Head Stability, Test of Skew and a pinching task.

What I did

  • Led development of the battery, learning Unity and the Varjo XR-3 independently and refining each protocol from the clinical literature.
  • Built an operator panel that walks the examiner through every test, with recording tied to the test so data cannot be lost or mislabelled.
  • Collected and analysed patient eye- and hand-tracking data in hospital settings, and presented findings to physicians and the lab.
  • Supervise the student researchers contributing to the project.
  • Applied for and won a $6,000 Schwartz Discover Grant from the University of Rochester to fund the research.

Results and status

  • Awarded a $6,000 Schwartz Discover Grant to carry out the research.
  • Validated on healthy controls and tested with a newly admitted stroke patient.
  • The recordings capture clinically expected signs such as corrective saccades, nystagmus and disconjugate eye movements.
  • Next: a study I am designing to validate the system against clinical vHIT goggles.
